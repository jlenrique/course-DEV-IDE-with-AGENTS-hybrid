## Design constraints

Carried from the Week 3 Box v02 pairing locks:

1. **Visual-led.** The image is the slide.
2. **Illustration / diagram / graphic only.** Photoreal is forbidden.
3. **Module learning objectives stay off-slide.** They inform the week's job; the
   four LO bullets are never printed on a card.
4. **No due date on the recording.** The assignment's Sunday deadline is a
   Canvas fact, not a slide fact.
5. **Zero interstitials.** The locks permit at most one; this material does not
   need it.
6. **One continuous case.** Maya Rivera / Harbor Bridge, carried from the
   week's own example — a through-line, not a cameo.
7. **Colleague-coach voice.** Supportive peer walking a task, not a lecturer.

### Evergreen rule (operator, 2026-09-13)

The Duolingo card is an **account of what the system implements**, not a tour
and not an in-lecture activity. The narration names no product features, no
pricing tier, and no interface affordance, because all three drift. Any
invitation to try it is optional, after the fact, and lives in the close.

### Anti-overwhelm rule (operator, 2026-09-13)

The rehearsal is **small-scale and manual**. No custom tooling or media assets
are expected. An interactive lecture qualifies. The student may act as **Wizard
of Oz** — hand-running anything a later prototype might automate. Cards 5, 6,
and 15 carry this explicitly; card 18 pays it off.

## Card 18 reference images

Captured 2026-09-13 from the signed-out Duolingo web placement flow (Spanish
from English), so the frames match what a student without a paid tier would
meet. No account was created and no personal identifier appears in either
frame.

Image files live in the **gitignored** staging bundle, not beside this file:
`course-content/staging/tracked/source-bundles/phs-620-w04-box-20260913/reference-images/`.
They are reproducible from the capture recipe below if this clone is lost.

| File | What it shows |
|---|---|
| `duolingo-A-attempt-with-scaffold.png` | Prompt "Write this in Spanish / two secretaries", three word-bank tiles (`difícil`, `secretarias`, `dos`), a hover-for-meaning hint, and an empty answer line — stimulus, scaffold, and invited attempt in one frame. |
| `duolingo-B-wrong-answer-correction.png` | The same item after an intentionally wrong answer (`dos difícil`), with the learner's attempt still visible above and "Correct solution: dos secretarias" returned below — a response to *that* attempt. |

Capture recipe (no account, no sign-in): open `duolingo.com`, answer the
placement prompt with "I can discuss most topics in detail", then choose "Find
my level". That skips beginner picture-matching and serves word-bank
translation items. Build a deliberately wrong answer and submit to reach the
correction frame.

Compositing note for the stills gate: pair the two frames side by side and
overlay the card-4 loop callouts across them. The wrong answer must stay
visible in frame — the attempt is half the point, and a bare correction panel
would not show that the system responded to something specific.

Honest limit: the placement flow returns a correction but no generated
explanation, and offers no "why" affordance. Narration must therefore claim
only that the system answers the specific attempt — not that it explains its
reasoning.
